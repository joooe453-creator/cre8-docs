# Share price and performance

Share price is the estimated value of one share of a fund, expressed in the accounting asset and derived from the fund's total assets (its net asset value, or NAV). It reflects assets, accrued income, fees, and share supply, and changes with markets and operations.

> Marketplace and example-fund holdings, performance, and history are simulated. CRE8 has not provided verified live fund performance records.

## What each figure means

| Figure | Meaning | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Share price | Valuation of one share based on available assets and price data | Not a guaranteed execution or withdrawal price |
| Estimated withdrawal | Proceeds estimated from current holdings and data | May differ from actual proceeds; check the minimum |
| Performance (7D / 30D / 90D / All) | Change in share price over a selected period, after fees | Period, fee treatment, and whether data is simulated; past performance is not a forecast |
| Protocol supply rate | An underlying market's interest or reward rate | Variable; not the fund's performance |
| TVL | Total value of assets in the fund | Growth may be new deposits rather than plan profit |

## Reading performance charts

Choose a period, then check data type, source, and timestamp. 7D, 30D, 90D, and All cover different intervals. Short-term changes can differ from longer-term outcomes.

For example, a share price after fees moving from 1.00 to 1.05 represents performance of about 5% over that period. New deposits can increase total assets and should not be counted as performance.

This illustrates reading a chart. Current examples use simulated share prices and fees, not onchain performance or evidence of executed trades.

**Maximum drawdown** describes the decline from a high to a low over the selected data interval. It depends on complete data for that period. It is not a future maximum-loss guarantee and differs from AI agent loss protection.

## Valuation versus proceeds

Asset prices, receipt values, interest, and available liquidity change. Valuation prices may differ from actual swap prices; withdrawals can incur slippage, trading costs, or third-party fees.

When you withdraw as assets, you may receive receipts. The displayed share price does not establish cash you can withdraw immediately.

## Missing or stale data

Missing prices, holdings, or market state may prevent valuation and block some deposits or new deployment. The last successful reading describes only its own timestamp.

Keep Unavailable, Stale, and incomplete periods visible in your interpretation. Missing values are not zero performance, and a protocol rate should not be substituted for fund performance.

## Supply rates and additional rewards

Check whether a market rate represents supply interest, additional token rewards, or a combination. Rewards may have eligibility, claim, lockup, or expiry conditions. Do not assume every fund receives them or includes them in its share price.

Supply rates change with utilization. A high rate does not establish available deposit capacity or withdrawal liquidity; review market status as well.

## Your own profit and loss

Different deposit, additional-deposit, and withdrawal times mean your results may differ from a fund's period performance. Check your deposits, share changes, actual withdrawals, and transaction costs.

Live historical sources, coverage, and presentation will be updated when confirmed. The prototype has historical-data processing, but implementation is not a live performance record. Cost basis and performance fees are address-specific; fund charts do not replace your actual net profit or loss.
