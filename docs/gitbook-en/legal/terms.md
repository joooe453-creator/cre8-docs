# CRE8 draft terms of use

Draft version: 2026-10-07-draft.8. Status: for product and legal review, not in effect. Available for Docs reading and general risk-notice flows; does not authorize live transactions.

This page adapts the public draft of that version for product reading. It is not the identical file in the original legal bundle or an effective agreement.

The draft addresses interface use and vault participation separately, based on current vault and operating permissions. CRE8 is an undeployed prototype. Example vaults and performance are simulated and do not accept live deposits. This is not a statement of licensing, regulatory approval, or a completed third-party audit.

The text below is for review. Before live publication, service providers and roles must be identified, open markets and required qualifications confirmed, and the corresponding documents finalized. This draft cannot substitute for unfinished requirements.

## Part I: provisions for finalization

### 1. Scope and documents

These terms address CRE8 websites, transaction-preparation tools, MCP, vaults, and personal-agent features. Offchain conversations and tasks, AI credits, and payments are separate from onchain creation, approvals, and vault fees. Availability depends on published market policies, deployment records, vault documents, and pre-transaction information. A displayed design, code, protocol name, or asset list does not establish availability or suitability for you.

Read these terms, [Before you use CRE8](before-you-use.md), [Risk disclosure](risk.md), [Eligibility and jurisdiction policy](eligibility.md), and the selected vault's documents in full. The live Privacy Policy will separately address data processing. Vault documents must at least identify the accounting asset, holdings, markets, operating caps, creator, Operator, Risk Council, fees, and exit routes.

These terms do not replace legally required offering, investment-management, financial-product, or specific service agreements. If a feature requires another agreement, investor qualification, or disclosure, that requirement must be completed first. A brief risk notice is not a substitute.

### 2. Documents, notices, and eligibility

Full documents remain available in Docs and the footer for reading and saving without a wallet. The vault list and individual vault pages show depositor notices; Create shows creator notices. The notice has an “I understand the risks” button and a “Don’t show this again” checkbox, with no Close or X. Escape returns to the previous page, or home if there is none, without recording understanding. Only checking the box and confirming saves the choice to hide that notice version for the same browser and wallet. A new version, device change, or cleared website data will show it again. Checking without confirming saves nothing. Confirmation does not accept live terms, verify eligibility, or approve a transaction. A separate deposit prompt asks whether you have read the Fund prospectus. Opening it stops that attempt; continuing only proceeds to preparation or preview, not signing or completing a deposit.

Legal documents are not in effect and the entity and markets remain undecided, so live creation and deposits remain closed. Agreements, consent, eligibility verification, and review required for live service must be completed separately. You must have legal capacity and qualify for the feature, with authority when acting for another person. Do not impersonate others or evade applicable limits. Browsing, wallet connection, or contract access does not establish legal permission.

Queries and exits for existing positions do not require acceptance of new commercial terms, but remain subject to law and technical conditions. Holdings sharing, AI tasks, and purchase confirmations are handled separately from general risk notices.

### 3. Platform, creator, and Operator roles

CRE8 provides creation, information display, transaction preparation, and related tools. Creators set assets, markets, caps, and a fixed performance fee, and appoint an Operator using its own signer for permitted operations. The platform receives entry and fund-operation fees, and both platform and creator charge exit performance fees at their respective rates. The arrangement cannot generally be characterized as completely neutral, free, or merely informational.

Vaults pool participant contributions and issue redeemable shares. Operators may continuously adjust holdings within authority. Legal classification and responsibilities depend on actual rights, control, fees, promotion, and applicable law. Labels such as DeFi, DAO, non-custodial, AI, or smart contracts do not automatically exclude financial, consumer, or other regulation.

The creator, Operator, service provider, and third parties are responsible according to their actual roles and legal duties. Using an underlying third-party protocol does not automatically release CRE8 from responsibility for its own integration, management, display, or promotion.

### 4. Private keys, AI, and transaction preparation

The platform does not hold your keys or seed phrase. The standard interface asks your wallet to sign and submit creation, deposit, redemption, and other transactions you initiate. MCP supplies unsigned transactions without signing or broadcasting. Operators use their own signers within scope. Protect wallets and devices; never enter keys or seed phrases into AI, support, or websites.

AI explanations, comparisons, estimates, and previews may be wrong, incomplete, or stale. Do not rely on output alone to decide a transaction. General explanations differ from actual Operator activity: after depositing into an authorized vault, the Operator may act within its mandate without requesting your individual signature again.

Informational material should not be treated as investment, legal, or tax advice tailored to you. If actual recommendations, management, or other activity create legal duties, this statement does not remove them. Data sent to AI or third parties, including wallet information, holdings, or questions, must match the actual feature and privacy notice. Optional sharing must not be bundled as necessary consent for deposits or redemptions.

### 5. Assets, shares, and authority

The current V2 uses custom nontransferable shares. Share allowances permit designated exit uses, not transfers. Shares reflect proportional participation under the rules, and value changes with assets, valuation, fees, and exits. They are not bank deposits and do not promise principal protection, fixed returns, or fiat withdrawal. Technical standards or names do not determine legal classification.

Accounting assets, markets, trading and pricing settings, creators, the Risk Council, and fee terms are fixed per vault. The Operator may be replaced through delayed governance. Basic vaults allow at most 7 distinct underlying entries, each with one designated destination. Debt and LP positions have separate flows and additional risk; they are not ordinary supply entries. Incomplete mixed-strategy features are not open. New markets or fixed core configurations require another vault under the current model. General terms acceptance does not authorize arbitrary changes.

Operator interfaces allow only bounded operations in designated markets, not arbitrary contract calls. Being Operator does not grant special withdrawal authority for holders; representative redemption still requires share authorization. Check deposit-token approval, share approval, and the actual transaction separately. Do not replace precise amounts with unnecessary large or long-lived allowances.

Deposited assets remain subject to contract rules, external protocols, and management permissions. Holding your keys does not mean that deposited assets can be recovered directly at will or that others have no operating authority.

An individual depositor cannot revoke or replace the whole vault's Operator. The creator may disable the agent immediately. A new agent requires at least 24 hours and valid activation conditions. Revoking wallet token or share allowances does not revoke authority over deposited assets. You may redeem your shares under exit conditions; immediate redemption at every moment is not promised.

### 6. Creation, deposits, and creator duties

Creation has preparation, deployment, and final activation stages. The first two neither contribute assets nor complete registration. Final activation contributes 100 accounting-asset units and applies the same 0.5% entry fee. Creator and ordinary-holder shares have no fixed time lock. While others remain, the creator must hold at least 2% of circulating shares, which limits maximum redemption and may constrain others' new deposits. The last actual holder may exit entirely. This is minimum ownership, not a separate margin deposit or principal guarantee.

Deposits first pay the entry fee and receive shares based on conservative valuation and actual contribution. Unused transaction budget is refunded to the payer. Nonempty-vault quotes may reserve a 1% execution buffer, which is refundable budget rather than an extra fixed fee. Check network, official vault address, accounting asset, amount, recipient, minimum shares, and deadline. Examples, design pages, and same-name assets do not replace verified addresses and onchain configuration.

Creators must truthfully explain strategy, rates, authority, interests, and known risks. Do not fabricate performance, promise protected principal or returns, or imply endorsement by the platform, auditors, or protocols. Creator and Operator promotion, management, and other activities must meet applicable qualifications. Interface review or technical approval is not legal permission.

### 7. Operating limits, pauses, and management

After each Operator deployment or supply operation, the current model checks position NAV caps and at least 5% idle accounting assets. Markets, valuations, and exits change later ratios. This is not a continuous 5% reserve or redemption guarantee.

Agent loss protection records positive NAV loss from capital actions under the same before-and-after prices. Default and platform maximum are 5%, observing approximately the last 24 hours with the oldest record potentially retained nearly an extra hour. The crossing action settles before the agent is disabled within that transaction. The 5% is not a hard cap on individual, cumulative, or total market loss, continuous monitoring, or guaranteed stop-loss. Personal and per-asset stops and profit targets have separate operation identities, replay protection, conditions, and triggers. Oracle, keeper, network, or liquidity issues may prevent target-price execution.

Creators may tighten caps immediately. Loosening requires onchain announcement and at least 24 hours. Creators and the Risk Council may pause activity under the rules; creator resumption after a Council pause requires at least 24 hours and valid risk conditions. Creators and Operators may reduce positions, and holder exits retain their conditions. Platform administrators may approve or revoke configurations for future creation without rewriting existing fixed markets.

Underlying upgrades, multisigs, pauses, blacklists, parameters, and receipts have their own rules. Review the permissions of each involved protocol. CRE8 limits do not imply equivalent third-party limits.

### 8. Fees, valuation, and conflicts

The current model has no management fee. These fees apply, with gas, slippage, trading, and protocol costs charged separately according to actual activity:

- **Entry fee: 0.5%.** Calculated on gross accounting assets actually used, rounded up in raw token units. Unused budget is not charged. The creator's 100-unit seed also pays it, giving a standard net contribution of 99.5 units.
- **Fund-operation fee: 0.1%.** Applies to chargeable capital deployments, reductions, and separate position operations. It is not an additional 0.1% for each click, approval, or ordinary holder redemption. Each fixed 365-day period from initialization has a cumulative cap of 1% of the latest valid pre-fee NAV; a new charge cannot exceed the remaining cap. A NAV decline does not recover paid fees. Collected fees are not necessarily below 1% of current NAV at every moment, and this is not a rolling 365-day cap.
- **Exit performance fee: fixed platform 10% plus creator 0–20%.** The creator rate is fixed at creation. The total is 10–30%, each applied to positive exit profit. The old 90/10 split is not used, and fee shares are not issued.

Each address has its own cost basis increased by net actual deposits. Partial exits allocate cost according to shares redeemed relative to that address's holdings, with raw-unit rounding. Cash-exit profit is the positive difference between actual liquidation proceeds and allocated cost. No positive difference means no performance fee for that exit. It is not netting across vaults, wallets, or all historical exits, or charging on a pooled unrealized high-water mark.

For example, cost basis 100, proceeds 120, and creator rate 10% produce profit 20. Platform and creator each receive 2, leaving 116 net. This ignores raw-unit rounding; 100 is net basis rather than the original payment including entry fee.

A reliably valued in-kind exit settles fees on that cohort's proportional asset value, sending the applicable proportion of those same assets to recipients instead of converting all assets first. Unpriceable fees are waived for an exit without reliable valuation to preserve the in-kind route. Cash fees enter a restricted fee-claim account. Recipients claim only their accrued fees and gain no authority to dispose of principal or burn holders' shares.

Entry and operation fees go to the platform. Both platform and creator benefit from positive exit profits and may have strategy, operating, and promotional conflicts. Read the vault's actual rates and recipient addresses.

### 9. Redemptions, in-kind exits, and limitations

Cash redemption processes proportional holdings for the shares exited and pays accounting tokens to the designated recipient. It does not promise bank cash or fiat withdrawal. The exiting holder bears liquidation costs and losses; NAV is not guaranteed proceeds. Minimum output, deadline, routes, oracles, external protocols, and liquidity affect results. One failed operation may revert the whole redemption.

In-kind redemption delivers proportional tokens, protocol receipts, and idle accounting assets. You may need to redeem receipts elsewhere. A market, transferability, immediate redemption, or recovery of principal is not guaranteed. This route reduces reliance on oracles and external cash withdrawals but still depends on contracts, gas, and transfer conditions. Blacklists, freezes, or insolvency may obstruct it.

Pauses, tighter caps, or disabling future-creation configurations must not be described as universally blocking all holder withdrawals. Reduction and exit routes remain, subject to creator ownership, approvals, and those limitations. No lockup does not guarantee liquidity or timing.

The vault permanently closes after the last actual shares exit, preventing a new depositor cohort. A final cash exit may also deliver remaining original assets or receipts; it cannot be described as exclusively accounting tokens. Liquidation, receipts, and share conversion may leave rounding differences. Exact conversion of every valuation into received assets is not promised.

### 10. Confirmation and safe use

Before signing, check network, contract, asset address, amount, recipient, allowance, minimum result, and deadline. General risk confirmation, Fund prospectus prompts, legally required acceptance, token approval, Permit, share authorization, Operator authority, and the actual transaction are distinct and must not be merged into an unclear confirmation.

Existing larger allowances may be reused and are not automatically revoked by entering a smaller amount. For unknown status, query the original identifier before deciding the next step, avoiding duplicate submission. Final onchain transactions usually cannot be unilaterally reversed by the platform; this does not waive lawful remedies.

Do not use tools for fraud, money laundering, terrorist financing, sanctions evasion, market manipulation, unauthorized access, infringement, or other unlawful acts. Legitimate vulnerability reporting and coordinated disclosure follow published rules. Good-faith research is not categorically a breach.

### 11. Data, third-party content, and taxes

The personal agent is a separate offchain feature with wallet-message sign-in. Tasks, conversations, credits, and payment records may be stored on servers. Necessary task content is sent to the configured AI provider. Holdings sharing is optional and separately selected. AI payments do not expand vault authority, include vault fees or keeper gas, or guarantee execution, returns, or live availability. Prices, credit charges, delivery, revisions, and refunds must appear in task/purchase confirmations. Unknown payments require reconciliation, not an assumption of failure and repayment. Refunds for undelivered paid tasks depend on the payment route and are not promised to be automatic, immediate, or unconditional.

Addresses, transactions, and holdings may remain public onchain and link to identity. Wallet use is not fully anonymous. The live Privacy Policy must explain actual data, purposes, processing grounds, retention, recipients, cross-border transfers, and rights channels. These terms must not compel optional analytics or marketing unrelated to necessary service.

External sites, protocols, wallets, RPC services, data sources, and issuers may have their own terms and qualifications. Branding, logos, integration, or display do not establish mutual endorsement. A third-party audit does not audit this vault or integration. Providers remain responsible under law for their own selection, integration, display, and explanation.

You and creators should understand tax and reporting duties for transactions, fees, and tokens. Providers' and other roles' own legal reporting and retention duties cannot all be shifted to you.

### 12. Service changes, suspension, and existing positions

Legal requirements, sanctions, security incidents, or other specific grounds may restrict a feature or new business. Where feasible and lawful, explain scope, reasons, effects, and assistance. Interface suspension does not erase onchain positions or guarantee third-party or direct-contract access.

Eligibility for new vaults, deposits, and additional risk must be handled separately from queries, exits, and remedies for existing positions. Refusal of new commercial terms alone must not hide lawful handling channels for existing assets. If law or sanctions restrict exits, provide feasible lawful queries and assistance; arbitrary circumvention cannot be promised.

Website updates do not arbitrarily change fixed onchain rates or configurations. Material changes to terms, risks, or data use need clear versions, effects, reasonable notice, and legally required renewed confirmation. Continued browsing cannot uniformly mean acceptance of all new permissions, charges, or uses.

### 13. Losses, liability, and statutory rights

Market prices, oracles, contracts, protocols, keys, networks, and liquidity may cause partial or total loss. CRE8 does not guarantee principal, returns, continuous service, a specific price, or exits at any moment. Simulations, audits, open source, and controls cannot eliminate all risks.

Risk disclosure does not assign every loss to the user. Damage caused by unlawful acts, breaches, or other legally actionable conduct of a provider, creator, Operator, or other role is assessed under applicable law and established causation. Automation, non-custody, general information, or third parties do not automatically excuse liability.

These terms do not exclude non-excludable liability, including applicable fraud, intentional wrongdoing, gross negligence, and consumer rights, or require waiver of regulatory complaints, investigative cooperation, or lawful judicial remedies. Future liability or indemnity provisions must be fair, specific, and lawful. This draft sets no arbitrary universal compensation cap or unlimited indemnity for all third-party claims.

### 14. Contacts, disputes, and other provisions

Live terms must identify the actual provider and usable contact and complaint channels, and determine governing law and disputes based on operations and service markets. No fictitious offshore entity, arbitration body, or contact address is designated. This draft does not establish an arbitration or court-selection agreement.

Chosen governing law cannot exclude applicable regulation, sanctions, financial rules, personal-data law, or mandatory consumer protections. Dispute terms must preserve non-excludable local-court and other rights. Invalid provisions are handled within applicable law without creating service eligibility not previously obtained.

Live terms should state language and document priority. Onchain configuration determines technical execution but code alone cannot deny statutory rights. Providers must disclose and address interface/contract inconsistencies instead of inducing trades through an incorrect interface.
